## **1. PURPOSE**

This policy establishes the framework for data management and privacy
protection at Sta. Ana Multipurpose Cooperative (SAMULCO). It ensures
compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173)
and other applicable privacy regulations while maintaining the
confidentiality, integrity, and availability of member and
organizational data.

## **2. SCOPE**

This policy applies to all:

-   SAMULCO personnel (employees, officers, Board members)

-   Third-party service providers and contractors

-   Business partners with access to SAMULCO data

-   Volunteers and interns

-   All data processing activities involving personal and sensitive data

## **3. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE FRAMEWORK**

### **3.1 Applicable Laws and Regulations**

-   **Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173)**

-   **Implementing Rules and Regulations of the Data Privacy Act**

-   **National Privacy Commission (NPC) Circulars and Issuances**

-   **Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Data Privacy Guidelines**

-   **Cooperative Development Authority (CDA) Data Protection
    > Requirements**

### **3.2 Privacy Principles**

SAMULCO adheres to the following data privacy principles:

-   **Transparency:** Clear information about data collection and use

-   **Legitimate Purpose:** Data processing for lawful and declared
    > purposes

-   **Proportionality:** Data collection limited to what is necessary

-   **Data Subject Rights:** Respect for individual privacy rights

-   **Accountability:** Responsibility for data protection compliance

-   **Security:** Appropriate safeguards for data protection

## **4. DATA CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM**

### **4.1 Classification Levels**

#### **4.1.1 PUBLIC DATA**

**Definition:** Information that can be freely disclosed without risk to
SAMULCO or individuals.

**Examples:**

-   Published financial statements

-   Marketing materials and brochures

-   Public announcements and news releases

-   General cooperative information

-   Job postings and recruitment materials

-   Educational and training materials

**Handling Requirements:**

-   No special security controls required

-   Can be shared with external parties

-   May be posted on public websites

-   Standard backup and retention applies

#### **4.1.2 INTERNAL DATA**

**Definition:** Information intended for internal use within SAMULCO
that could cause minor harm if disclosed.

**Examples:**

-   Internal policies and procedures (non-confidential)

-   Organizational charts and contact lists

-   Meeting minutes (non-confidential)

-   Internal communications and memos

-   Training materials for employees

-   Vendor contact information

**Handling Requirements:**

-   Access limited to SAMULCO personnel

-   Cannot be shared externally without authorization

-   Standard password protection for electronic files

-   Secure disposal when no longer needed

-   Regular access review and monitoring

#### **4.1.3 CONFIDENTIAL DATA**

**Definition:** Sensitive information that could cause significant harm
to SAMULCO or individuals if disclosed.

**Examples:**

-   Member personal information

-   Financial records and transaction data

-   Loan applications and credit information

-   Employee personal and HR records

-   Business plans and strategies

-   Vendor contracts and agreements

-   Audit reports and findings

-   Legal documents and correspondence

**Handling Requirements:**

-   Access on a need-to-know basis only

-   Formal access authorization required

-   Encryption required for electronic transmission

-   Secure storage with access controls

-   Confidentiality agreements for external sharing

-   Enhanced monitoring and audit logging

-   Secure destruction procedures

#### **4.1.4 HIGHLY CONFIDENTIAL DATA**

**Definition:** Extremely sensitive information that could cause severe
harm if disclosed or compromised.

**Examples:**

-   Social Security Numbers and government-issued IDs

-   Banking account numbers and financial credentials

-   Medical and health information

-   Criminal background check results

-   Investigation reports and findings

-   Legal proceedings and litigation documents

-   System authentication credentials

-   Encryption keys and security certificates

**Handling Requirements:**

-   Strictly limited access with formal approval

-   Multi-factor authentication required

-   End-to-end encryption for all storage and transmission

-   Segregated storage systems with enhanced security

-   Continuous monitoring and alerting

-   Special handling and disposal procedures

-   Executive approval for external sharing

-   Detailed audit trails and reporting

### **4.2 Data Classification Responsibilities**

#### **4.2.1 Data Owners**

-   Classify data according to its sensitivity and business value

-   Define access rights and handling requirements

-   Approve access requests and permissions

-   Monitor data usage and access patterns

-   Ensure compliance with classification requirements

-   Review and update classifications periodically

#### **4.2.2 Data Custodians**

-   Implement technical and administrative controls

-   Ensure proper storage and backup procedures

-   Monitor system access and usage

-   Implement security controls as specified by data owners

-   Maintain audit logs and reports

-   Execute data retention and disposal procedures

#### **4.2.3 Data Users**

-   Handle data according to its classification level

-   Respect access controls and permissions

-   Report suspected privacy breaches immediately

-   Follow proper procedures for data sharing

-   Maintain confidentiality of sensitive information

-   Complete required privacy training and certification

## **5. PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION**

### **5.1 Types of Personal Data**

#### **5.1.1 Personal Information**

**Definition:** Information about an identified or identifiable
individual.

**Examples:**

-   Full names and addresses

-   Contact information (phone, email)

-   Date and place of birth

-   Occupation and employment information

-   Family relationships and status

-   Educational background

#### **5.1.2 Sensitive Personal Information**

**Definition:** Personal information about an individual\'s private life
or could prejudice safety or security.

**Examples:**

-   Government-issued identification numbers

-   Financial account information and credit data

-   Health records and medical information

-   Religious beliefs and affiliations

-   Political opinions and memberships

-   Sexual orientation and preferences

-   Criminal records and background checks

-   Biometric data (fingerprints, photos)

### **5.2 Lawful Basis for Processing**

SAMULCO processes personal data based on the following lawful grounds:

#### **5.2.1 Consent**

-   Freely given, specific, informed consent from data subjects

-   Clear opt-in mechanisms for data collection

-   Ability to withdraw consent at any time

-   Documentation of consent records

-   Regular consent renewal for ongoing processing

#### **5.2.2 Contract Performance**

-   Processing necessary for membership agreements

-   Loan applications and credit assessments

-   Service delivery and account management

-   Payment processing and collections

-   Contract administration and compliance

#### **5.2.3 Legal Obligation**

-   Compliance with BSP reporting requirements

-   Tax reporting and government filings

-   Anti-money laundering (AMLC) obligations

-   Court orders and legal processes

-   Regulatory investigations and inquiries

#### **5.2.4 Legitimate Interest**

-   Fraud prevention and detection

-   Security monitoring and incident response

-   Business analytics and improvement

-   Marketing and member communications

-   Risk management and assessment

## **6. DATA SUBJECT RIGHTS**

### **6.1 Right to Information**

**Description:** Data subjects have the right to know what personal
information is collected and how it\'s used.

**SAMULCO Obligations:**

-   Provide clear privacy notices at the point of collection

-   Explain purposes of data processing

-   Identify data sharing arrangements

-   Specify retention periods

-   Provide contact information for inquiries

**Implementation:**

-   Privacy notices displayed prominently

-   Multilingual notices when appropriate

-   Regular updates to reflect changes

-   Staff training on privacy notice requirements

-   Documentation of notice delivery

### **6.2 Right of Access**

**Description:** Data subjects can request copies of their personal
information.

**SAMULCO Obligations:**

-   Provide access to personal information upon request

-   Deliver information in understandable format

-   Respond within statutory timeframes (15 days)

-   Verify identity before disclosure

-   Provide information free of charge (with reasonable limits)

**Implementation:**

-   Formal request procedures established

-   Identity verification protocols

-   Secure delivery mechanisms

-   Tracking and monitoring of requests

-   Staff training on access procedures

### **6.3 Right to Correction**

**Description:** Data subjects can request correction of inaccurate or
incomplete information.

**SAMULCO Obligations:**

-   Correct inaccurate personal information promptly

-   Complete incomplete information when requested

-   Notify third parties of corrections when applicable

-   Maintain audit trail of corrections

-   Respond within statutory timeframes

**Implementation:**

-   Correction request procedures

-   Data quality validation processes

-   Change management controls

-   Third-party notification procedures

-   Documentation of correction activities

### **6.4 Right to Erasure (\"Right to be Forgotten\")**

**Description:** Data subjects can request deletion of personal
information under certain circumstances.

**SAMULCO Obligations:**

-   Delete personal information when legally permitted

-   Consider retention requirements before deletion

-   Notify third parties of deletion requests

-   Maintain records of deletion activities

-   Balance erasure rights with legal obligations

**Implementation:**

-   Erasure request evaluation procedures

-   Legal retention requirement checks

-   Secure deletion methods and verification

-   Third-party notification processes

-   Audit trails and documentation

### **6.5 Right to Data Portability**

**Description:** Data subjects can request their personal information in
portable format.

**SAMULCO Obligations:**

-   Provide personal information in structured format

-   Use commonly used electronic formats

-   Enable direct transmission to other organizations when feasible

-   Respond within statutory timeframes

-   Verify identity and authorization

**Implementation:**

-   Data export capabilities and formats

-   Identity verification procedures

-   Secure transmission methods

-   Documentation of portability requests

-   Staff training on portability requirements

### **6.6 Right to Object**

**Description:** Data subjects can object to processing of their
personal information.

**SAMULCO Obligations:**

-   Stop processing when objection is valid

-   Evaluate objections against legitimate interests

-   Provide alternative services when possible

-   Document objection decisions

-   Notify data subject of outcome

**Implementation:**

-   Objection evaluation procedures

-   Processing impact assessments

-   Alternative service options

-   Decision documentation requirements

-   Communication protocols

## **7. DATA RETENTION AND ARCHIVAL**

### **7.1 Retention Schedule**

#### **7.1.1 Member Records**

-   **Active Members:** Retained during membership period plus 7 years

-   **Inactive Members:** 5 years after account closure

-   **Loan Records:** 10 years after full payment

-   **Transaction Records:** 5 years from transaction date

-   **Compliance Records:** As required by regulations (typically 5-10
    > years)

#### **7.1.2 Employee Records**

-   **Personnel Files:** 5 years after employment termination

-   **Payroll Records:** 5 years after tax year end

-   **Performance Records:** 3 years after employment termination

-   **Training Records:** 3 years after completion

-   **Background Checks:** 1 year after employment decision

#### **7.1.3 IT and Security Records**

-   **System Logs:** 1 year minimum, 3 years for security incidents

-   **Email Archives:** 3 months operational, 1 year backup

-   **Access Records:** 3 years minimum

-   **Security Incidents:** 5 years after resolution

-   **Backup Data:** Follow primary data retention schedules

#### **7.1.4 Financial and Business Records**

-   **Financial Statements:** Permanent retention

-   **Audit Reports:** 7 years

-   **Contracts:** 7 years after expiration

-   **Insurance Records:** 7 years after policy expiration

-   **Legal Documents:** Permanent or as specified by legal counsel

### **7.2 Archival Procedures**

#### **7.2.1 Data Encryption**

-   All archived data must be encrypted using approved methods

-   Encryption keys managed separately from archived data

-   Key rotation and management procedures followed

-   Decryption capabilities maintained throughout retention period

-   Documentation of encryption methods and key management

#### **7.2.2 Archive Storage Locations**

-   **File Server:** Central server storage with access controls

-   **External Devices:** Encrypted portable storage in secured vault

-   **Cloud Storage:** Approved cloud services with data encryption

-   **Off-site Storage:** Geographically separate secure facility

-   **Multiple Copies:** Redundant storage to prevent data loss

#### **7.2.3 Archive Access Controls**

-   Limited access to authorized personnel only

-   Formal approval process for archive access

-   Audit logging of all archive access activities

-   Regular access review and certification

-   Emergency access procedures for business continuity

### **7.3 Data Destruction**

#### **7.3.1 Destruction Methods**

-   **Electronic Data:** Secure deletion with overwriting or degaussing

-   **Physical Media:** Shredding, crushing, or incineration

-   **Cloud Data:** Verified deletion from cloud provider systems

-   **Backup Media:** Physical destruction or certified wiping

-   **Documentation:** Certificate of destruction for audit purposes

#### **7.3.2 Destruction Procedures**

-   Systematic identification of data eligible for destruction

-   Approval process for destruction activities

-   Verification of destruction completion

-   Documentation and audit trail maintenance

-   Environmental compliance in destruction methods

-   Approval requirements for sensitive data sharing

-   Audit logging of cross-departmental data access

-   Training on proper data sharing procedures

-   Regular monitoring and compliance audits

-   Compliance with legal obligations and court orders

-   Proper verification of authority and legal basis

-   Documentation of all mandatory disclosures

-   Formal data sharing agreements required

### **8.3 Cross-Border Data Transfers**

#### **8.3.1 Transfer Requirements**

-   Assessment of destination country privacy laws

-   Adequate level of protection verification

-   Data transfer impact assessments

-   Appropriate safeguards implementation

-   Documentation of transfer legal basis

#### **8.3.2 Safeguards for International Transfers**

-   Standard contractual clauses where applicable

-   Certification schemes and codes of conduct

-   Encryption and security measures for data in transit

-   Regular monitoring of transfer arrangements

## **9. PRIVACY IMPACT ASSESSMENTS**

### **9.1 When PIAs are Required**

#### **9.1.1 Mandatory Assessments**

-   New data processing activities involving sensitive personal
    > information

-   Significant changes to existing processing activities

-   Implementation of new technologies or systems

-   High-risk processing activities

-   Large-scale processing of personal information

#### **9.1.2 Assessment Triggers**

-   Processing of children\'s personal information

-   Biometric or genetic data processing

-   Location tracking and monitoring

-   Automated decision-making systems

-   Data matching and profiling activities

### **9.2 PIA Process**

#### **9.2.1 Assessment Components**

-   **Description of Processing:** Purpose, data types, legal basis

-   **Necessity Assessment:** Proportionality and necessity analysis

-   **Risk Assessment:** Identification and evaluation of privacy risks

-   **Mitigation Measures:** Controls and safeguards to address risks

-   **Consultation:** Input from stakeholders and data protection
    > officer

#### **9.2.2 Risk Evaluation Criteria**

-   **Likelihood:** Probability of privacy risks occurring

-   **Impact:** Severity of consequences to data subjects

-   **Vulnerability:** Weaknesses in current controls

-   **Threat Level:** External and internal threat assessment

-   **Residual Risk:** Risk level after mitigation measures

### **9.3 PIA Documentation and Review**

#### **9.3.1 Documentation Requirements**

-   Comprehensive assessment reports

-   Risk registers and mitigation plans

-   Stakeholder consultation records

-   Review and approval documentation

-   Implementation monitoring reports

#### **9.3.2 Ongoing Monitoring**

-   Regular review and updates of PIAs

-   Monitoring of implemented mitigation measures

-   Incident tracking and lessons learned

-   Effectiveness assessment of controls

-   Continuous improvement of assessment process

## **11. INCIDENT MANAGEMENT**

### **11.1 Privacy Breach Definition**

A privacy breach includes any unauthorized or accidental:

-   Collection, use, disclosure, or retention of personal information

-   Access to personal information by unauthorized persons

-   Loss, theft, or destruction of personal information

-   Modification or alteration of personal information

-   Failure to secure personal information adequately

### **11.2 Breach Response Procedures**

#### **11.2.1 Immediate Response (0-24 hours)**

-   **Detection and Confirmation:** Verify and confirm the breach

-   **Containment:** Stop ongoing unauthorized access or disclosure

-   **Assessment:** Initial assessment of scope and impact

-   **Notification:** Internal notification to management and DPO

-   **Documentation:** Begin documentation of incident details

#### **11.2.2 Investigation Phase (1-72 hours)**

-   **Investigation Team:** Assemble appropriate response team

-   **Root Cause Analysis:** Determine how and why breach occurred

-   **Impact Assessment:** Assess risks to affected individuals

-   **Evidence Preservation:** Preserve evidence for investigation

-   **Regulatory Notification:** Notify NPC within 72 hours if required

#### **11.2.3 Notification and Remediation**

-   **Individual Notification:** Notify affected individuals when
    > required

-   **Stakeholder Communication:** Inform relevant stakeholders

-   **Remediation Measures:** Implement measures to address breach

-   **Monitoring:** Monitor for additional unauthorized activity

-   **Lessons Learned:** Identify improvements and preventive measures

### **11.3 Breach Notification Requirements**

#### **11.3.1 Regulatory Notification**

-   **National Privacy Commission:** Within 72 hours of discovery

-   **Other Regulators:** As required by specific laws

-   **Law Enforcement:** For criminal activity or significant breaches

-   **Insurance Providers:** For covered incidents

-   **Business Partners:** As required by contracts

#### **11.3.2 Individual Notification**

-   **High Risk Threshold:** When breach likely to result in high risk

-   **Timing:** Without undue delay, typically within 30 days

-   **Content:** Clear description of breach and recommended actions

-   **Method:** Most appropriate and secure communication method

-   **Documentation:** Record of all individual notifications sent
