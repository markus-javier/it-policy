## **1. PURPOSE**

This policy establishes the framework for managing changes to Information and Communication Technology (ICT) systems, infrastructure, and projects at Sta. Ana Multipurpose Cooperative (SAMULCO). The goal is to ensure that standardized methods and procedures are used for efficient and prompt handling of all changes, minimizing the impact of change-related incidents upon service quality, and consequently improving the day-to-day operations of the cooperative. It specifically outlines the creation of change management plans for ICT projects.

## **2. SCOPE**

This policy applies to all ICT changes and projects within SAMULCO, including but not limited to:
- Hardware and software installations, upgrades, or removals
- Network infrastructure changes
- Core banking system and financial application updates
- New IT project deployments and transitions
- Security patch management and configuration changes
- System migrations and integrations

## **3. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES**

### **3.1 Change Advisory Board (CAB)**
- Reviews, assesses, and approves or rejects Requests for Change (RFCs).
- Evaluates the impact, risk, and resource requirements of proposed changes.
- Ensures that all proposed changes align with SAMULCO's strategic objectives and do not pose unacceptable risks to business continuity or security.

### **3.2 Change Manager (ICT Department Head or Designee)**
- Coordinates and oversees the entire change management process.
- Convenes and chairs CAB meetings.
- Ensures all changes are documented, adequately tested, and implemented according to approved plans.

### **3.3 Change Requester (Project Manager / System Owner)**
- Initiates the formal Request for Change (RFC).
- Provides comprehensive details, business justification, risk assessment, and impact analysis for the proposed change.
- Develops the initial change management plan for their specific project.

### **3.4 Implementation Team**
- Executes the approved change according to the schedule.
- Performs testing, validation, and quality assurance.
- Prepares and executes rollback or remediation plans in case of failure.

## **4. CHANGE CATEGORIZATION**

All changes must be categorized to determine the required level of review and approval:

- **Standard Changes:** Pre-authorized, low-risk, and commonly occurring changes that follow a documented, established procedure (e.g., routine system maintenance, standard software installations).
- **Normal Changes:** Changes that require formal CAB review and approval before implementation (e.g., core system upgrades, deployment of new business applications, significant network changes).
- **Emergency Changes:** Highly critical changes needed immediately to resolve a major incident, address a critical security vulnerability, or prevent a significant business impact. These require expedited approval from the ICT Department Head and relevant executive management.

## **5. CHANGE MANAGEMENT PROCESS FOR ICT PROJECTS**

### **5.1 Request for Change (RFC) Submission**
All non-standard changes must be initiated via a formal RFC submitted to the ICT Department. The RFC must include:
- Description of the change and business justification.
- Identification of systems, services, and stakeholders affected.
- Proposed timeline and resource requirements.

### **5.2 Change Management Plan Development**
For all Normal Changes and major ICT projects, a comprehensive Change Management Plan must be developed. This plan must include:
- **Implementation Plan:** Step-by-step procedures for deploying the change.
- **Testing Plan:** Procedures for verifying the change in a non-production environment prior to deployment, and post-deployment validation steps.
- **Communication Plan:** Strategy for notifying affected end-users, stakeholders, and support teams about the change, expected downtime, and new features.
- **Rollback / Back-out Plan:** Detailed steps to revert the system to its previous state if the change fails or causes unacceptable negative impacts.

### **5.3 Review and Assessment**
- The Change Manager conducts an initial review of the RFC and Change Management Plan for completeness.
- The CAB evaluates Normal Changes based on the submitted plan, assessing potential impact, risk, resource availability, and scheduling conflicts.

### **5.4 Approval**
- **Standard Changes:** Automatically approved if following established procedures.
- **Normal Changes:** Require formal CAB approval documented in the change management system or meeting minutes.
- **Emergency Changes:** Require immediate, documented approval from the ICT Head or designated executive, with a post-implementation review by the CAB.

### **5.5 Implementation and Deployment**
- Approved changes are scheduled during designated maintenance windows whenever possible to minimize business disruption.
- The Implementation Team executes the change in strict adherence to the approved Implementation Plan.
- Any deviation from the plan must be escalated to the Change Manager immediately.

### **5.6 Post-Implementation Review (PIR)**
- Following the deployment of Normal and Emergency changes, the Change Manager will conduct a PIR.
- The PIR assesses whether the change achieved its objectives, reviews any incidents caused by the change, and evaluates the effectiveness of the change management process.
- Lessons learned are documented to continuously improve future ICT projects and change management procedures.

## **6. COMPLIANCE AND ENFORCEMENT**

- Unauthorized changes (changes implemented without proper categorization, review, and approval) are strictly prohibited and may result in disciplinary action.
- The ICT Department will maintain an audit trail of all RFCs, approvals, and post-implementation reviews to ensure compliance with this policy and relevant regulatory requirements.
